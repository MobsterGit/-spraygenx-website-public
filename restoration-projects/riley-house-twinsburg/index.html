Initial Condition

This was not a routine repaint.

The Riley House showed widespread exterior coating failure across its historic wood siding and architectural details. Large areas had lost adhesion, leaving weathered substrate exposed. Windows, trim transitions, siding joints, chimney-adjacent surfaces, and previously patched areas required close examination before any coating work could be treated as a normal production repaint.

On an older structure, preparation is also investigation. Every loose edge, open joint, soft area, failed repair, and moisture pathway can change the restoration plan. The objective is not simply to make the building look newly painted. The objective is to stabilize vulnerable surfaces, protect historic material where practical, and create a coating system that respects the condition of the structure beneath it.

Advanced peeling and loss of adhesion across broad siding areas
Exposed and weathered wood requiring preservation-minded preparation
Deteriorated window trim, corners, joints, and prior repair locations
High-detail conditions around the chimney, roofline, gables, and architectural trim
Unforeseen conditions requiring continuous communication with the property trustees

Restoration Standard

Three principles guiding the work

Document before covering

Condition records preserve the reasoning behind repairs and help owners understand what was discovered as preparation advanced.

Prepare to the substrate

Coating success depends on the actual condition of the wood, joints, repairs, and moisture pathways—not merely the appearance of the remaining paint.

Communicate discoveries

Historic work frequently reveals unforeseen conditions. Timely owner communication protects the project, schedule, and preservation priorities.
