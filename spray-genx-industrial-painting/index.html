Industrial workflow

The coating system follows the environment

Before selecting a product, the project team must understand the substrate, existing coating, corrosion, contamination, exposure, cleaning requirements and finish goal. Interior ceilings, exterior metal, machinery and concrete floors do not share one universal preparation or coating system.

Industrial work may also require off-hours scheduling, phased production, lift and scaffold access, dust or overspray containment, equipment protection and coordination with plant personnel. These operating conditions belong in the scope and estimate.

  • Identify the substrate and existing coating
  • Determine cleaning and preparation
  • Define primers and finish system
  • Coordinate access and shutdowns
  • Protect production assets and inventory
  • Document exclusions and acceptance criteria
Industrial painting is risk management

A coating failure can begin with an overlooked contaminant, incompatible primer, unaddressed moisture, insufficient access or a schedule that does not allow proper curing. The best production plan reduces those risks before mobilization.

Estimating information

What Spray GenX needs to evaluate an industrial project

Useful early information includes facility location, building use, approximate quantities, photographs, substrate, current condition, desired finish, height, access, production schedule and areas that cannot be shut down.

Drawings and specifications should be provided when available. On renovation work, a site visit may be necessary to identify contamination, coating failure, access restrictions and protection requirements that photographs cannot show.
