Restoration Practice

Work guided by the condition of the building.

Historic and severely weathered exteriors require more than generalized scraping and repainting. The scope must respond to the substrate, failed repairs, open joints, moisture pathways, architectural details, and the owner’s preservation priorities.

Condition documentation

Photographs and field notes establish what was found, where deterioration is concentrated, and why specific repairs or coating decisions are necessary.

Preservation-minded preparation

Failed material is removed while sound historic fabric is retained where practical, with repairs matched to the actual condition of the wood and trim.

Clear owner communication

Newly exposed conditions are documented and discussed so repair priorities, schedule, cost, and long-term protection remain aligned.
