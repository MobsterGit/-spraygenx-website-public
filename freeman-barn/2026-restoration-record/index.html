Project Summary

Renewing the Barn.
Preserving Its Story.

The restoration addressed structural deterioration at the lower siding, failed exterior coatings, vulnerable window openings, a binding original sliding door, loose roof panels and weathered historic signage. Where practical, original wood and operating features were retained and repaired rather than discarded.

Restoration Ledger

Repairs &
Preservation Methods

01 · Original siding and lower perimeter

Approximately four inches of severely decayed siding ends were removed around the building. Because those ends had contained the original lower fastening points, continuous 2¼-inch × 6-inch ground-contact pressure-treated perimeter lumber was attached to the lower framing. Remaining original siding was resecured with countersunk GRK flathead exterior screws. Missing and deteriorated corner wood was rebuilt. Damaged battens were repaired and loose exterior nails reset.

Custom-bent aluminum drip-edge flashing was installed over the new perimeter detail to shed water away from the siding connection and underlying framing.

02 · Wood treatment and finish coatings

The exterior received clear PrimeRX treatment, including repeated heavy applications where wood was particularly weathered. The barn's exterior siding and architectural trim were refinished in the historic red-and-white scheme. The treatment and preparation beneath the finish are central to the long-term preservation work.

03 · Original windows and drainage corrections

Every original wood window, including upper-story windows, was completely reglazed: old glazing removed, glazing surfaces prepared with oil-based primer, new compound installed and two finish coats applied. Deteriorated upper window assemblies were opened and repaired. A previous repair containing newspaper beneath putty-like material was discovered; the contractor assessed this as a likely moisture-retention issue. Upper ledges received flashing, with deliberate drainage openings left to let water escape.

The three front replacement windows were cleaned, their tracks cleared and operating parts lubricated; a displaced upper sash was reseated. Damaged screens were addressed. An original side pocket/sliding window that had been painted shut was freed, removed, restored, reglazed, repainted, reinstalled and fitted with a handle and small lock.

04 · Main sliding barn door

Cracked and rotted support wood had allowed the large original sliding door to sag and bind. The door was raised temporarily using wood blocking and leverage while its support was repaired and reinforced with treated lumber and structural fasteners. The existing track was secured again. The door was returned to free sliding operation; the result was documented on video. Additional latch and protective-cover work was performed.

05 · Metal roof

Loose metal roof panels were resecured with EPDM-sealing-washer roofing screws. The roof was recoated with Henry's 555 fibered aluminum coating to renew the protective finish.

06 · Associated historic society work

An existing decorative wooden post was restored and repainted in red with black-and-white accents at the request of a decorator preparing the historical society property for the Christmas season.

Historic Sign Painting

Mail Pouch
Lives On.

The north-gable CHEW MAIL POUCH TOBACCO advertisement and TREAT YOURSELF TO THE BEST slogan were carefully restored in colors selected as close modern matches to the historic artwork: Lemon Twist yellow, Tricorn Black and Pure White.

The large lettering received two primer coats and two finish coats for each color. The small original “HW 87” painter's mark, attributed to celebrated Mail Pouch painter Harley E. Warrick, was traced by hand four separate times to preserve the surviving handwriting, form and placement. These four tracings refer to the signature only—not the large lettering.
